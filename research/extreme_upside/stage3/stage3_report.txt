------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. ANSWER TO THE CENTRAL QUESTION (short)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Can pre-event information identify stocks with a meaningfully higher probability of +50/100/200 % over 5/10/20
sessions, and does it survive genuinely out-of-sample? YES for ranking, with three strong qualifications.

The ranking survives out-of-sample:
  - Primary target, frozen models, untouched test 2021-2025: base rate 0.580 % (9,300 events in 1.60 M
    security-days).
  - The top 1 % of each day by the best pre-selected model (full-A random forest) has an event rate of 7.74 %
    [6.83, 8.53], i.e. 13x the base. The top 10 % captures 68 % of all events. PR-AUC is 0.0596 [0.0540, 0.0667]
    against 0.0037 unconditional; ROC-AUC is 0.915.
  - 2026 YTD (separate): base 0.913 %, top 1 % 10.7 %, PR-AUC 0.081.
  - All 27 targets show the same pattern (secondary endpoints, section 6.3).

Qualification 1 - the signal is mostly one dimension:
  - It is "cheap, volatile, illiquid micro-cap". Low price, high ATR / realised volatility, low dollar volume, low
    market cap and low float are BH-significant, have the expected sign, are block-consistent and replicate in
    2021-2025. But they are largely the same thing.
  - A volume-only logistic model reaches test P@1% 7.02 % (vs 7.74 % for RF) and PR-AUC 0.0460.
  - The pre-registered rule for "multivariate value beyond simple baselines" (Amendment 2) requires the test
    PR-AUC CI to lie above the best baseline's CI. RF scored [0.0540, 0.0667] against volume LR's
    [0.0413, 0.0550]. The CIs overlap by 0.001, so the rule is NOT met: INCONCLUSIVE.

Qualification 2 - several hypotheses failed or reversed:
  - Interactions (H10), fundamentals, short interest and float add nothing measurable beyond price / volume
    features.
  - The momentum (H7) and short-interest (H3) hypotheses are contradicted or unsupported; see section 10.

Qualification 3 - predictive is not tradable:
  - Among the top 1 % of model scores in the test period, the median price is $0.66 and 62 % are below $1.
  - 27 % trade under $100k per day.
  - Only 29 % of the Family-A events are confirmed by the close at t+10 (Family B), and only 53 % by any close in
    the window (B-max). Almost half the excursions exist only as an intraday high.
  - Nothing in this report is a trading result. A trading simulation (spread, slippage, halts, liquidity, sizing)
    is a separate later stage.

Overlap and episode convention (brief item 8):
  - Labels are defined on every security-day, so consecutive days before one move are all positive. On average
    one A primary episode contains 5.8 positive security-days (24,371 / 4,207).
  - Episode rule: an episode starts at the first positive day t of a security. Positive days of that security in
    the next h sessions are merged into it. The next episode can only start >= h sessions after the previous
    start.
  - Consequences:
    a. Security-day rates overstate the number of independent moves about 6-fold. Episodes count moves.
    b. Rows are strongly dependent, so every CI in this report uses a cluster bootstrap by security. Naive
       p-values are never used.
    c. Training rows were purged / embargoed: 60 calendar days (about 41 sessions >= h + 20) before every period
       boundary, so no label window crosses into validation or test.

  - Any use of these scores as probabilities needs era recalibration, which was not done here: no recalibration
    was pre-registered.

  - No profitability claim is made or implied.

10. CONCLUSION - WHICH HYPOTHESES HAVE EVIDENCE (deliverable 10)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence rule (protocol section 5), all four required:
  (1) BH-significant;
  (2) expected sign, or a meaningful two-sided effect;
  (3) consistent in >= 4 of 5 blocks (4 dev blocks + test; fewer where the data start later);
  (4) replicates in the untouched 2021-2025 test.

EVIDENCE - all four criteria met. NOT five independent discoveries: they measure overlapping aspects of one
"cheap / volatile / illiquid micro-cap" dimension (sub-additive in H10).
  H8 low price (lpx) [A]
     - Effect: top vs bottom decile RR 0.01 [0.00, 0.02], i.e. a 2.12 % vs 0.023 % rate.
     - n: 4.19 M rows, 13,346 events.
     - OOS 2021-25: RR 0.01 [0.00, 0.02], 3.06 % vs 0.02 % (1.60 M rows, 9,300 events).
     - Blocks: 4 / 4.
     - Robustness: RR 0.01 in A-strict, A-exADR and A-fund, dev and test (F.1).
     - Limitations: partly mechanical (tick size and discreteness at sub-$1 prices; possible bad-tick highs).
  H5 high volatility (atr_p; rv20 as secondary) [A]
     - Effect: RR 48.9 [34.3, 77.0], 2.09 % vs 0.043 %.
     - OOS: RR 104.8 [60.4, 277.2], 3.46 % vs 0.03 %.
     - Blocks: 4 / 4. AUC 0.871 dev, 0.890 test.
     - Robustness: RR 37-123 in every sensitivity universe and period (F.1).
     - Limitations: volatility is partly the price effect; this is the largest single predictor.
  H9 low dollar volume (ldv; two-sided) [A]
     - Effect: RR 0.03 [0.02, 0.05].
     - OOS: 0.09 [0.05, 0.14].
     - Blocks: 4 / 4.
     - Robustness: RR 0.03 dev / 0.08-0.09 test in every sensitivity universe (F.1).
     - Limitations: collinear with price; the test decile shape peaks in d2 (1.51 %) rather than d1.
  H1 low market cap (lmcap) [B]
     - Effect: RR 0.01 [0.00, 0.01].
     - n: 2.61 M rows, 5,395 events.
     - OOS: RR 0.00 [0.00, 0.00], 3.21 % vs 0.00 % (1.15 M rows, 6,495 events).
     - Blocks: 4 / 4.
     - Robustness: same sign in all 27 targets; the sensitivity universes do not apply (B is its own universe).
     - Limitations: B requires a Morningstar snapshot; adds nothing in the models.
  H4 low float (lfloat) [C, 2011+]
     - Effect: RR 0.04 [0.02, 0.09].
     - n: 1.75 M rows, 3,243 events.
     - OOS: RR 0.11 [0.04, 0.27] (1.10 M rows, 6,010 events).
     - Blocks: 3 / 3 available + test.
     - Robustness: same sign in all secondary targets.
     - Limitations: float is a stale ratio times PIT shares; collinear with market cap; adds nothing in the
       models. float_ds (H4s) is weaker: dev RR 0.45, q 0.036; test 0.66 [0.41, 0.98].

NO EVIDENCE / CONTRADICTED:
  H2 relative volume (+)
     - Not significant in dev (RR 0.94 [0.84, 1.01], q 0.17).
     - Test RR 0.87 [0.77, 0.98] is in the OPPOSITE direction.
     - Both volume extremes are elevated (U-shape); the "high relative volume -> more events" hypothesis is not
       supported.
  H3 short interest (+)
     - si_ds is not significant in dev (0.76 [0.41, 1.37]) or test (0.83 [0.52, 1.23]).
     - The secondary variable days-to-cover replicates in the OPPOSITE direction: low dtc -> more events, RR 0.21
       dev / 0.16 test. This most likely reflects liquidity, not squeezes.
     - The squeeze hypothesis is not supported.
  H7 momentum (+)
     - Contradicted: extreme 20-day LOSERS have the highest rate. RR top/bottom 0.49 dev, 0.56 test, consistent
       in every block; r5 is the same.
     - Both tails are elevated. The positive-momentum hypothesis as stated is rejected for this target.
  H6 gap / intraday (two-sided)
     - gap: dev RR 1.39 [1.30, 1.48], consistent in 4 blocks, but test RR 1.01 [0.95, 1.07] -> FAILED
       replication of the top-vs-bottom contrast. The test shape is symmetric (d1 1.56 %, d10 1.58 %, middle
       0.2-0.4 %).
     - intraday replicates (0.69 dev, 0.85 test), but the effect is small and also U-shaped.
     - Status: a directional gap effect is NOT supported. "Large absolute gaps / intraday moves of either sign"
       is a descriptive pattern, not a tested hypothesis.
  H10 interactions
     - No evidence: GBM minus additive LR on validation +0.0002 [-0.0056, +0.0060].

INCONCLUSIVE:
  - Multivariate value beyond simple baselines: test PR-AUC of the pre-selected full-A RF vs the best
    single-group baseline (volume LR) overlaps by 0.001. Clearly better than price-only and momentum-only.
  - Fundamentals, short interest and float as incremental predictors in B, C and D: differences are within the CI
    in both periods. This does not show they are useless; it shows no measurable increment in these samples.

Overall answer:
  - Information available at the close of t ranks stocks for +50/100/200 % excursions far better than chance.
  - This survives an untouched five-year test and 2026 YTD, across all 27 targets.
  - The information is essentially "how cheap, volatile and illiquid the stock already is". Simple price / volume
    models capture most of it.
  - Almost half of the primary-target events are intraday-high-only excursions in sub-$1 stocks. That is a
    statement about predictability, not about an exploitable strategy.

Known limitations (global):
  - 20 % fixed-hash security sample. The universe classification has an unresolved category (Stage 2, option A).
    The weekly universe pass misses securities that lived < 1 week.
  - Old-era data sparsity (miss20 22-29 % before 2015) and probable bad prints in 2006-09 penny stocks.
  - Model CIs use 60 resamples (2026 YTD: 30), so the CI tails are approximate. The evidence rule is
    conservative (non-overlap); a paired difference test was not pre-registered and was not run.
  - No recalibration. GBM tree count hit the grid minimum in C and D. D has only two years of training data.
  - The univariate test-period p-values were not separately BH-adjusted. All replicated effects have p at the
    bootstrap floor (0.005), which survives any BH adjustment over 18 tests.
  - Family A uses intraday highs; no execution, spread, halt or slippage modelling (by design: a later stage).
